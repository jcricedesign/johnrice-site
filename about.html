About

I've always been interested in how things work — and why they don't.

That curiosity drives my design work. I like figuring out what's unclear, what's unnecessary, and what can be improved.

I was raised in the Pacific Northwest, around people who built things — houses, boats, companies. Practical work and clear thinking. That mindset shows up in how I design: no drama, no fluff, just solid solutions.

I studied advertising and illustration at Seattle Central College. Since then I've worked across print, web, product design, and digital systems. The tools change. The fundamentals don't.

I started in print production, where a mistake went to press anyway and you owned it. That taught me discipline. When print began to fade I taught myself HTML and CSS and took a pay cut to move into interactive work — not glamorous, but necessary. That decision led through agencies and startups and eventually into product roles at Xbox, Microsoft, and Mozilla, on systems used by millions.

I've always loved books, history, and art. David Macaulay's The Way Things Work, Lego manuals, punk rock, role-playing games — all of it shaped how I think about systems and storytelling. My design sensibility owes a lot to Swiss and Bauhaus principles: grid, hierarchy, restraint. I'd rather remove something than add it. Removing things is usually where a product starts to sing.

What I'm good at

Ambiguity. The stretch where nobody agrees what the problem is yet, the research is contradictory, and someone has to decide what gets built first and defend it. I'm comfortable being the person who goes and talks to customers, comes back with an inconvenient answer, and turns it into a plan a team can execute.

How I work with engineers

Closely, and through implementation rather than up to it. I deliver production-ready specs and follow work through release and post-release. Show rather than tell — I'd rather put a prototype in front of someone than describe it.

Where I am

Lynnwood, Washington, just north of Seattle. Available for contract roles, on-site in the Seattle area or fully remote.
