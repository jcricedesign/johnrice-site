Shuffletube

Broadcast television as something you can watch, control, save, manipulate, and remix.

Shuffletube is a hands-on experiment built around a Raspberry Pi 5, a dual over-the-air TV tuner, and free local broadcasts. The goal is deliberately open: explore what television becomes when the receiver is yours and the software is programmable.

Live video surface
4.1KOMO

Prototype: ← / → changes the sample channel · Space toggles the light control layer

Receive

Free local OTA television through a Hauppauge WinTV-dualHD tuner. Two independent tuners are available to the Pi.

Control

Tune, pause, buffer, preview, record, and eventually browse what is on without building a traditional cable-guide interface.

Remix

Capture broadcast clips and frames, save them locally, replace audio, combine sources, and treat television as creative material.

Current experiment

Get the television on screen efficiently.

The first hardware test is working: the Pi recognizes both tuners, scans local ATSC stations, records native broadcast streams, plays them fullscreen over HDMI, and can switch channels programmatically. The current investigation is the cleanest low-overhead rendering path for live video with a lightweight interface above it.

  • Hauppauge dual tuner recognized natively by Linux.
  • Strong local OTA reception established with an amplified antenna.
  • Native transport-stream recording and fullscreen playback proven.
  • 1080i MPEG-2 playback tested successfully on Raspberry Pi 5.
  • Lightweight fullscreen TV chrome prototyped separately from the video renderer.
